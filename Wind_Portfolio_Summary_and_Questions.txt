                            WIND PORTFOLIO PERFORMANCE OPTIMIZATION
                         EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & QUESTION FRAMEWORK CHARTER
=================================================================================================================

1. BUSINESS PROBLEM
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Across our 1 GW synthetic wind asset portfolio, power generation suffers from an estimated 5%–8% annual 
generation gap relative to optimal potential. Operational telemetry reveals that during approximately 14% of 
turbine-operating hours, turbines generate at least 10% less power than expected under comparable wind and 
weather conditions. This ongoing shortfall leaves tens of millions of kilowatt-hours and significant potential 
energy revenue uncaptured due to unidentified technical, aerodynamic, and control-setting defects.


2. STRATEGIC OBJECTIVES
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Business & Financial Objective: Identify, explain, and prioritize controllable technical losses across the 
  fleet to recover 2%–3% of lost Annual Energy Production (AEP). For a 1 GW portfolio operating at a 38% capacity 
  factor (~3.33 TWh/year baseline), this represents an annual recovery of 66.6 to 99.9 GWh/year.

* Analytics Objective: Construct a high-precision digital power baseline model that predicts expected turbine 
  power output with an accuracy target of R² >= 0.90, while isolating external grid curtailment and downtime from 
  addressable technical losses.


3. TARGET VARIABLE DEFINITIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Primary Target (Regression): Expected_Power_kW
  A continuous output metric representing the exact active power (in kW) a turbine should generate given real-time 
  meteorological conditions (wind speed, wind direction, ambient temperature, pressure, and air density).

* Secondary Target (Classification): Underperformance_Flag
  A binary indicator (1 or 0) triggered whenever a turbine experiences a controllable power loss under active, 
  non-curtailed operating conditions:
  
  Underperformance_Flag = 1 IF:
    (Expected_Power_kW - Actual_Power_kW) / Expected_Power_kW > 0.10
    AND Turbine_Available == True
    AND Grid_Curtailment_Flag == False
    AND Wind_Speed >= Cut_In_Wind_Speed (e.g., 3.0 m/s)
  ELSE 0


4. BUSINESS QUESTIONS FRAMEWORK (5x5 FRAMEWORK)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. DESCRIPTIVE QUESTIONS (What happened?)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. What is the current capacity factor and total energy yield across all turbines in the portfolio over the last 12 months?
2. What percentage of total operational hours fall into the >10% power loss category?
3. How is lost generation distributed across different wind farms, sub-regions, and turbine models?
4. What are the baseline environmental conditions (average wind speed, direction, temperature) during peak underperformance periods?
5. How often do planned maintenance outages overlap with high-wind resource windows?

B. DIAGNOSTIC QUESTIONS (Why did it happen?)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Which specific component anomalies (e.g., yaw misalignment, pitch system drift, gearbox over-temperature) correlate most strongly with the >10% generation gap?
2. How much underperformance is caused by controllable technical losses versus uncontrollable weather variations or grid curtailment?
3. Are specific turbine models or serial numbers suffering from systematic software or control-setting degradation?
4. Does wake interference from upstream turbines account for unexplained localized power drops in specific wind directions?
5. How much generation is lost to curtailment events enforced by the transmission system operator versus internal safety shutdowns?

C. PREDICTIVE QUESTIONS (What will happen?)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. What is the forecasted power generation (Expected_Power_kW) for the portfolio over the next 1-hour, 6-hour, and 24-hour horizons?
2. Which turbines are most likely to experience a >10% power deficit in the upcoming 7 days based on real-time SCADA trends?
3. Can early temperature and vibration signatures predict gearbox or main-bearing degradation before a mechanical failure occurs?
4. How much total energy loss (MWh) do we project for the portfolio over the next quarter if current underperformance trends remain unaddressed?
5. Which environmental conditions are most likely to trigger curtailment or derating over the next 48 hours?

D. PRESCRIPTIVE QUESTIONS (How can we make it happen?)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Which specific turbines should be prioritized for immediate field maintenance to maximize recovered MWh and financial return?
2. What optimal pitch and yaw control adjustments will eliminate recurring aerodynamics-driven underperformance?
3. When should maintenance schedules be set to minimize revenue loss during low-wind periods?
4. How should technical resources be allocated between software/control recalibration versus physical component repairs to hit the 2–3% recovery target?
5. What automated operational threshold should trigger an inspection alert before a derated turbine causes a critical energy deficit?

E. COGNITIVE QUESTIONS (How can the system adapt and learn?)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. How can the system continuously refine its baseline power-curve models as turbines age and components wear over time?
2. Can the model autonomously distinguish between temporary environmental anomalies (e.g., blade icing or bug fouling) and long-term mechanical degradation?
3. How can real-time operator overrides and feedback from field technician repairs be integrated to improve fault classification accuracy?
4. How can the model balance short-term revenue optimization against long-term turbine health to recommend real-time derating strategies?
5. How can the framework translate complex SCADA multi-sensor anomalies into natural-language action plans for field engineering teams?
